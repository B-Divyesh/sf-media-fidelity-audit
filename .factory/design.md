# Media Fidelity Audit — visual thesis

## Direction: paper-cut diorama

This product is about the physical reassurance of putting a family archive away
intact. The landing page therefore depicts a small archival shelf made from
layered, cut-paper folders: a warm source envelope, a dark camera reel, and a
green archive box connected by a visible thread. The layers make the comparison
legible without pretending that media files are visually inspected. It is a
quiet, tactile counterweight to a clinical backup dashboard.

## Tokens

* **Background:** `#f6f0e5` (uncoated paper), surface `#fffaf1`, ink
  `#1d2927`, muted ink `#52605b`.
* **Archive navy:** `#173b49`; **source terracotta:** `#b84c35`; **thread
  gold:** `#b77916`; success `#176948`; warning `#965c12`; danger `#a52f28`.
  These have been chosen for 4.5:1 body-text contrast against their specified
  surfaces.
* **Type:** system serif (`Iowan Old Style`, Georgia) for the human promise;
  system UI sans for procedures and results. This avoids a downloaded font,
  keeps the artifact fast, and gives the evidence a field-notebook voice.
* **Spacing:** a four-pixel base, with 16/24/40/64 px groups. Broad matte
  margins keep the scan instructions calm.
* **Light/dark:** light is archival paper; dark becomes a charcoal workbench
  with the same terracotta, paper and green semantic signals.

## Interaction and motion

Buttons compress like paper labels on press. The diorama enters upward by 12px
once (220ms), while results use an opacity change only. At
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, every transition is removed and the hero is
static. There are no loops or flashing effects.

## Asset plan and provenance

`site/src/assets/archive-diorama.webp` is an original generated editorial
illustration: layered paper folders, camera reel and archive carton on a warm
paper backdrop, with no words, brand marks, people, or copied artwork. It is
generated by the factory-image tool from the prompt recorded in the handoff and
converted to WebP under 300 KB. Small UI marks are native CSS, not borrowed
iconography.
